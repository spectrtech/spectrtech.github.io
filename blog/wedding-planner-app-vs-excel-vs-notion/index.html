Wedding Planner App vs Excel vs Notion: What Each Is Good For (Honest Comparison)

SpectrTech Guides · September 16, 2026

Every couple starts with the tool they already have: a spreadsheet, a Notion page, a notes app. That is the right instinct — and for a 40-person wedding it may be all you need. The question is where each tool stops being enough, and that depends on the task more than the tool. Here is the comparison by task.

When a spreadsheet is enough

Under about 60 guests, one venue, no seating chart beyond “family near the front”: a spreadsheet with a guests tab, a budget tab and a vendors tab is the right tool. You know it, your partner knows it, and there is nothing to learn. Add three columns to the budget (estimated, quoted, final) and a contingency line, and you have most of what a planner would give you.

Where Notion wins

If you already live in Notion, its relations between databases are the closest a general tool gets to a planner: vendor ↔ budget line ↔ payment. Templates exist, though most are cosmetic. The cost is set-up time — a couple of evenings — and it is slow on a phone with a big guest database.

Where both break: 100+ guests and a seating chart

The seating chart is the task that sends people looking for a planner. At 100+ guests with families, exes, kids and a venue with fixed tables, the rules stop fitting in your head, and a grid of cells cannot enforce them. This is the one place where “a tool built for one job” earns its keep: guests carry their tags, the chart reads them, and a late decline empties the chair automatically.

What to look for in a planner

  • Import from a spreadsheet — you should not retype 140 names.
  • Seating that drags guests onto chairs and respects group / keep-apart rules.
  • Budget with estimate → quote → final, not one number.
  • Clear answer to “where is my data stored” and “what happens if I stop paying”.
  • Export: printable seating chart, guest list with table numbers, PDF invitations if included.
